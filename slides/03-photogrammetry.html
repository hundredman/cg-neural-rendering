Classical Method

Photogrammetry

Take many overlapping photos of an object → find matching points across images → compute 3D geometry.
1
Capture photos from many angles
100–1000 photos of the same object. Every part must appear in at least 2–3 images.
2
Find matching feature points
Software detects the same corner/edge across multiple photos to figure out camera positions.
3
Reconstruct a 3D mesh
Triangulate matching points in 3D space → build a surface → project photos as texture.
4
Export to 3D software
The resulting mesh can be used in games, VFX, or VR — just like a hand-modelled asset.
What it gets right
✅
Works today — used in film, archaeology, surveying, game asset creation.
✅
Portable — just a camera. Used to digitize Notre-Dame before the fire.
Where it struggles
❌
Shiny / transparent surfaces — mirrors, glass, metal all confuse feature matching.
❌
Fuzzy / thin objects — hair, fur, plants, smoke — no clean surface to match.
❌
View-dependent lighting — the mesh bakes in the lighting from the shoot day. Relighting is nearly impossible.
The key limitation
Photogrammetry builds a mesh. A mesh is rigid — it can't represent soft, fuzzy, or view-dependent appearances well.
