Evaluation

Pros & Cons

✅ What neural rendering does well
🌿
Handles complex materials
Hair, smoke, glass, fur — things that break photogrammetry are handled naturally.
📸
Photos only — no special hardware
A phone camera is enough. No LiDAR, no depth sensor, no expensive equipment.
💡
View-dependent lighting
Reflections and highlights look correct from every angle — the network learns how light behaves.
⚡
3DGS: real-time rendering
3D Gaussian Splatting achieves 30+ fps — fast enough for VR and live applications.
❌ Current limitations
🔒
One model = one scene
Training a NeRF gives you only that scene. New scene = train again from scratch.
✏️
Hard to edit
You can't easily move an object, change a texture, or animate — the scene is "baked in."
🏃
Struggles with motion
Moving objects cause blurring and ghosting. Capturing a running person or flowing water is still very hard.
💾
Large file size (3DGS)
Millions of Gaussians can take hundreds of MB to GBs per scene — too large for most phones today.
Bottom line Neural rendering excels at capturing appearance from photos, but still lags behind traditional methods for editing, animation, and large-scale scenes.
